# Satire and Easter eggs

The joke targets institutional incentives, accounting theatre, and the gap between public-service language and revenue pressure. Characters and institutions are fictional. Do not imply that a real officer, politician, or bank employee committed a made-up offence. Recognizable allusions work through props and situations; the game does not need real logos or copied footage.

“Too Big to Park” grants a collectible newspaper, not an enormous payout. Cosmetic jokes must not secretly alter detection or challenge fairness. Easter eggs appear in a discovery notebook with short English text and optional French localization. No discovery is required for progression.

## Tone and delivery

Use short environmental jokes: a sticker on a printer, a line on a receipt, a supervisor's euphemism. Keep frequent UI language functional. A news ticker saying “Recovery fund ahead of target; definition of recovery under review” can be funny once; the cash tooltip should still explain cash.

Example fictional radio lines: “Excellent work. The graph is now moving in the approved direction.” “The ministry has requested fewer complaints, preferably without fewer fines.” “Your camera is hidden. Your invoices are not.”

The premise intentionally exaggerates fines as a national rescue strategy. Real French radar proceeds have several allocations, not simply the officer's spending account. Swiss banking intervention is a historical inspiration for a fictional joke, not a claim that ordinary speeding enforcement caused or funded it. See [sources](09-sources.md) for the narrow factual references.

## Implementation rules

Each discovery has a stable ID, trigger, location, localized text keys, reward type, and source/invention note. Keep one encounter per run unless the player explicitly inspects it again. Use original voices and fictional likenesses. Record asset licenses in the register. Political commentary should remain understandable without knowing a specific scandal; recognition adds a second layer.
